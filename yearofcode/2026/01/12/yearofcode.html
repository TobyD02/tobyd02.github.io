Year of Code!

Jan 12, 2026

This year, I’ve decided to commit to what I’m calling a “Year of Code”. I find that I often get to the end of each year feeling as if I’ve done absolutely nothing - so this is my attempt to track my development projects and progress. The goal is to, each week, document what projects I’m working on, what I’ve learned, what I plan to do next, etc… Anything that is relevant – then hopefully by the end of the year I’ll be able to look back and see what I’ve accomplished.

Beyond just keeping track of my progress, there are some loose goals that I want to set myself:

1. Explore some new domains

I spend every day at work building the same kinds of systems, It feels like there would be no benefit to doing the same thing during my free time. Instead, I want to touch on some new frontiers - not only to expand my knowledge, but also to dip my toes into things that I maybe would not have been exposed to otherwise. Who knows - I might find something that really clicks and pursue it further.

There are some domains that I’ve been interested in and played around with in the past but never really stuck with (Game Development, Audio DSP, Practical Applications of Machine Learning, etc…). This leads on quite nicely into my next goal…

2. Finish some projects!

One of the major pitfalls I always run into when working on personal projects is that I never seem to finish them. My original plan for this year was to stick to a project a week, forcing myself to finish 52 projects – however, I quickly discovered that this was extremely ambitious (hence why I’m sticking to just documenting whatever progress I make). Despite this, I think the core idea was solid. It would be really nice to have a list of completed projects, perhaps even deployed on production servers, that I could showcase. So this is my goal – finish atleast 1 project… just 1. Obviously, I’d like to aim for more (1 a month would be amazing), but I want to be realistic. Also, I don’t want to enforce some abitrary number that ends up killing my passion, I’m much more interested in exploring new things that just finishing any random project.

3. Write more

This is kind of the whole point – somewhere to dump all my thoughts, ideas, etc… I don’t expect to be writing every day, but at a minimum I want to keep it to 1 a week: my “Year of Code” updates. With theses just being documentation of my weekly projects, it will require minimum effort to keep up with. Then hopefully, once the habit builds, I might start writing more.
